What Size Crate Does a Cavalier King Charles Spaniel Need?

Measure, add four inches, and plan for the adult dog from day one. A fit planner, sizing table and travel rules for Cavalier puppies and adults.

Home›Blog›What Size Crate Does a Cavalier Need?

Published: 2026-10-14 · Cavalier King Charles Spaniel Zone

Figuring out what size crate a Cavalier King Charles Spaniel needs feels like it should be simple, yet most owners I talk to have stood in a store aisle staring at a 24-inch and a 30-inch box, worried they are about to get it wrong. I get it: you want a crate that feels like a safe den, not a cage, and you do not want to buy three of them before your puppy turns one. The good news is that the answer comes from your own dog's measurements, not from guesswork, and it takes about five minutes with a soft tape.

Quick answer: Measure your Cavalier standing, from the tip of the nose to the base of the tail, and sitting, from the floor to the top of the head. Add four inches to each. The crate's interior needs to meet both numbers.

For a typical adult Cavalier of 13 to 18 pounds and 12 to 13 inches at the shoulder, that usually lands on a 24-inch crate for compact dogs and a 30-inch crate for longer or taller ones. For a puppy, buy the crate that fits the expected adult size and shrink it with a divider panel.

How big does a Cavalier King Charles Spaniel get?

The breed standard describes an adult Cavalier as 12 to 13 inches tall at the withers and 13 to 18 pounds, with the body only slightly longer than the dog is tall. Those numbers set the starting range for crate size, but your own dog's tape measurements are what decide it.

A breed standard reprinted in Showsight Magazine's judging guide describes the correct Cavalier as a small, well-balanced dog of 13 to 18 pounds and 12 to 13 inches in height that "approaches squareness". The length from point of shoulder to point of buttock is slightly longer than the height at the withers. The same text notes that the distance from withers to elbow equals the distance from elbow to ground. That detail matters later if you ever need an airline crate.

The Wikipedia breed article gives a similar average of 13 to 18 pounds (5.9 to 8.2 kg). It also lists a wider 12 to 18 pound range in its summary box. In real homes, plenty of pet Cavaliers sit outside the show range. The judging guide itself warns about "a tendency toward bigger dogs". So if your Cavalier is a solid 20 pounds, you are not doing anything wrong. You simply need to measure rather than rely on the breed label.

Why weight is the weakest guide to crate size

Crate boxes print weight ranges because weight is easy to know. A crate, though, has to fit the dog's length and height. Two Cavaliers can both weigh 16 pounds while one is noticeably longer in the back or taller through the head. Treat the weight on the box as a rough cross-check and the tape measure as the decision.

The two measurements that decide everything

  • Length: with your dog standing on all four feet, from the tip of the nose to where the tail joins the body. Do not include the feathered tail.
  • Height: with your dog sitting up, from the floor to the top of the head. Ears flat on a Cavalier rarely add height, but measure to whatever is highest.

How much room should you add to the measurements?

Add about four inches to both the length and the height measurement. That gives your Cavalier room to stand, turn around and lie down without the crate feeling roomy enough to use one end as a toilet.

The Chewy measuring guide takes height while the dog is sitting, from the base of a front paw to the top of the head, and takes length standing, from tail base to nose tip. It then suggests adding four inches to both. It also recommends sizing up whenever your dog falls between two crate sizes. The test after assembly is simple: if your dog can comfortably stand up, lie down and turn around inside, the crate is big enough.

Why "bigger is kinder" is not quite true

It is natural to want to give your dog lots of space. Cavaliers are sociable, cuddly dogs, and a small box can feel mean. But a crate works because it feels like a den. A crate far longer than the dog leaves a sleeping corner at one end and spare floor at the other, and for a puppy that spare floor easily becomes the bathroom. The four-inch margin is a comfortable den, not a punishment.

Is a 24-inch or 30-inch crate right for a Cavalier?

A 24-inch crate suits a compact adult Cavalier whose nose-to-tail-base length is about 20 inches or less. A 30-inch crate suits longer dogs and is the safer choice when you are buying for a puppy whose adult size is uncertain. Always check the listed interior dimensions, because the size in a crate's name is not always the space inside.

Do not forget the height check

Owners often check length and forget height. Crate heights vary between brands, and a Cavalier sitting up with its head raised needs its sitting height plus four inches of headroom. If your dog has to duck to sit, the crate is too small even if the length works.

Small home, big crate?

If floor space is tight, a crate that is too small is not the fix. Look for a crate with a smaller footprint for the same interior, such as one with a side door against a wall. Our guide on whether Cavaliers are good apartment dogs covers how to fit a dog's daily routine into a small home.

What size crate does a Cavalier puppy need?

Buy the crate that fits the expected adult size, then use a divider panel so your puppy gets only the space they need today. That saves you buying two or three crates in the first year and keeps house-training on track.

Advice from Humane World for Animals' crate training guide is direct: the crate should be large enough for your dog to stand up and turn around. If your dog is still growing, choose a crate size that will fit their adult size. The guide notes that some crates adjust as the dog grows. Some shelters also rent crates so you can trade up until your puppy reaches adult size.

How to estimate the adult size of a Cavalier puppy

You cannot measure a dog that does not exist yet, and growth charts for individual puppies are guesses. The most honest method is to ask your breeder to measure one or both parents the same way (nose to tail base standing, head height sitting) and use the larger set of numbers. If that is not possible, plan for the upper end of the breed standard and choose the 30-inch class with a divider. A crate that is slightly generous with the divider in place costs you nothing. One that is too small means buying again.

Moving the divider as your puppy grows

Re-measure every two to three weeks. When your puppy's length plus four inches is more than the space behind the divider, move it back one notch. Signs that you are late: your puppy curls up tightly because they cannot stretch out, or sits with the head pressed against the roof.

Does a travel crate need to be a different size?

Yes. Airline crates follow their own formula, and that formula often gives a different result from the home-crate rule. Wire home crates are also not accepted for air transport.

The IATA pet container requirements (Live Animals Regulations, edition 52, January 2026) give minimum interior sizes based on four body measurements:

  • A: length from the tip of the nose to the base of the tail.
  • B: height from the ground to the elbow joint.
  • C: width across the shoulders or widest point, whichever is greater.
  • D: height in a natural standing position, from the floor to the top of the head or the ear tip, whichever is higher.

Container length is A + ½B, width is C × 2, and height is D plus the height of the bedding. The same document says each animal must have room to stand, sit erect, lie in a natural position and turn about normally. It also states that containers made entirely of wire mesh must not be used for air transport. Airlines can set their own rules on top of these, so check with your carrier before you buy.

A handy shortcut for Cavaliers

Because the breed standard says withers-to-elbow equals elbow-to-ground, the elbow height (B) of a well-proportioned Cavalier is roughly half of the shoulder height. For a dog that measures 12 to 13 inches at the shoulder, that is around 6 to 6.5 inches. Half of that adds only about 3 inches to the length, slightly less than the home-crate margin. Measure your own dog's elbow rather than assume it, though, as pet Cavaliers are not always built to the standard.

Step-by-step: measuring your Cavalier and choosing the crate

Pick a calm moment, ideally after a walk, and have someone help hold a treat at nose level.

  1. Gather a soft tape measure, treats and a notepad. A metal tape is awkward on a moving dog.
  2. Measure length standing. Nose tip to the base of the tail, tape following the line of the back. Take it twice and keep the larger number.
  3. Measure height sitting. Floor to the top of the head while your Cavalier sits upright looking at the treat.
  4. Add four inches to each. These are your minimum interior length and height.
  5. For a puppy, repeat with the parents' numbers or plan for the larger class.
  6. Read the product page for interior dimensions, not just the name on the box. If either number is short, size up.
  7. Do the fit test at home. With the door open and a treat inside, watch your dog stand, turn and lie flat. A Cavalier that lies stretched on its side should not be pressed against both ends.
  8. Note today's numbers. Puppies get re-measured every two to three weeks.

Comfort inside the right-sized crate

Size is only half of a happy crate. A washable bed that does not eat up headroom helps, and so does a spot away from radiators and direct sun. Short sessions at first build the idea that the crate is a good place. Crate time is also a calm moment to check the coat for mats behind the ears. How long can a Cavalier stay in the crate?

Even a perfectly sized crate is for short stretches. Humane World for Animals says puppies under six months should not stay crated for more than three or four hours at a time. It also says adult dogs should not be crated for more than four to five hours during the day.

The same guide gives a second puppy rule of thumb: no longer than their age in months plus one hour. When the two rules differ, follow the shorter limit. Cavaliers were bred as companions and usually want to be near their people. If your days are long, plan a dog walker or a midday visit rather than a bigger crate. A correctly sized crate is a bedroom, not a place to spend the whole day.

When to talk to your vet

If your Cavalier suddenly hates lying down in the crate, scratches at the neck or seems uncomfortable resting the head, do not assume the crate is the problem. Check with your vet.

Common Cavalier crate sizing mistakes

  • Buying by weight alone. A box that says "up to 25 lbs" tells you nothing about whether a long-backed Cavalier can stretch out.
  • Measuring to the tail tip. The feathered tail adds inches that the crate does not need. Stop at the tail base.
  • Trusting the name on the box. The size in the product name is not always the space inside, so read the listed interior dimensions.
  • Buying the puppy-sized crate. It fits for a few weeks, then you buy again. Buy for the adult and use a divider.
  • Going far too big without a divider. Extra floor at one end can become a toilet area during house-training.
  • Forgetting headroom. Length fits but the dog cannot sit upright. Check height as well.
  • Using the home wire crate for flying. Airline rules call for a different, non-wire container sized by the IATA formula.

Frequently asked questions about Cavalier crate size

Is a 24-inch crate big enough for a Cavalier King Charles Spaniel?

Often, yes, for a compact adult. By the four-inch rule, a 24-inch crate fits a Cavalier whose nose-to-tail-base length is about 20 inches or less. It must also be tall enough for the sitting head height plus four inches. Check the interior dimensions; if your dog is longer or taller, choose the 30-inch class.

Should I buy a 30-inch crate for my Cavalier puppy?

If you do not know the adult size, a 30-inch crate with a divider panel is the safer single purchase. Set the divider so the space is about your puppy's length plus four inches, and move it back as they grow.

How do I measure my Cavalier for a crate?

Measure length standing, from the tip of the nose to the base of the tail, and height sitting, from the floor to the top of the head. Add four inches to each to get the minimum interior length and height.

Can a crate be too big for a Cavalier?

Yes, especially for a puppy. A crate much longer than the dog leaves room to sleep at one end and soil at the other, which slows house-training. Use a divider until your Cavalier is fully grown.

What size airline crate does a Cavalier need?

Under the IATA formula, interior length is nose-to-tail-base plus half the elbow height, width is twice the shoulder width, and height is standing head or ear height plus bedding. All-wire crates are not allowed for air transport, and airlines may add their own rules.

How much does an adult Cavalier weigh?

The breed standard describes 13 to 18 pounds at 12 to 13 inches tall. Many pet Cavaliers are a little outside that range, which is why measuring beats choosing a crate by weight.

Getting the Cavalier crate size right

If you have been going back and forth between crate sizes, that care is exactly what your Cavalier needs from you. The answer to what size crate a Cavalier King Charles Spaniel needs comes down to two numbers plus four inches each. For many adults that means a 24-inch crate, and for longer dogs or puppies with a divider it means a 30-inch crate. Measure today, run the crate fit planner, and re-check your puppy every few weeks. Then let the crate become the calm, cozy den it is meant to be.
